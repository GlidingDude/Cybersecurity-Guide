WHAT IS CYBERSECURITY?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications, devices, and information from unauthorized access, damage, theft, and disruption. As more of our lives move online, cybersecurity has become important for students, businesses, governments, and ordinary internet users.

Confidentiality

Only authorized people should have access to sensitive information. Passwords, encryption, and access controls help protect confidentiality.

Integrity

Information should remain accurate and should not be changed by unauthorized users.

Availability

Systems and information should remain accessible when legitimate users need them.

COMMON CYBER THREATS

Understanding common threats makes it easier to recognize suspicious activity before it becomes a serious problem.

Phishing

Attackers create fake emails, messages, websites, or login pages designed to trick users into revealing information.

CLICK TO LEARN MORE
Common warning signs include urgent requests, suspicious links, unexpected attachments, fake login pages, and unfamiliar senders.

Malware

Malware is software created to damage systems, steal information, spy on users, or perform other unauthorized actions.

CLICK TO LEARN MORE
Examples include viruses, worms, spyware, trojans, and other malicious programs. Keeping software updated can reduce security risks.

Ransomware

Ransomware can prevent users from accessing files or systems and may demand payment from victims.

CLICK TO LEARN MORE
Regular offline or otherwise protected backups can help organizations recover without relying entirely on the attacker.

Social Engineering

Social engineering manipulates people into revealing information or performing actions that compromise security.

CLICK TO LEARN MORE
Attackers may pretend to be employees, friends, delivery services, technical support, or other trusted contacts.

HOW A PHISHING ATTACK WORKS

Many cyber attacks follow a predictable process. Learning the process can help you recognize an attack before reaching the final stage.

1

Contact

The attacker sends an email, message, or other communication.

2

Impersonation

The message pretends to come from a trusted person or organization.

3

Urgency

The victim is pressured to act quickly without checking the message.

4

Interaction

The victim clicks a link, opens an attachment, or provides information.

5

Compromise

The attacker may gain access to an account or sensitive information.
